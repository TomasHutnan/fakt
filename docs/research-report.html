What Chytrák does, and the gap

How it plays

  • Each question shows 10 options, always 5 correct and 5 wrong.
  • One device for 1 to 5 players. Each player answers the same question, then passes the phone on.
  • Each correct pick adds points. An app-store aggregator lists 1 point for the first correct pick and 15 for all five. A wrong pick wipes what you earned on that question.
  • The game runs to a target score that can be changed in settings.
  • It is a native iOS and Android app. The site is only a landing page.

What it leaves open

  • The site claims "hundreds of questions". Extra questions are paid.
  • No custom question sets and no import.
  • Content is Czech. Nothing Slovak-specific was found.
  • One question format only.
  • No play on separate phones and no big-screen mode.

Not found: prices, the number of free questions, and the exact end condition.

The mechanic closely matches the board game Smart10 (sold in Czech as Desítka): ten answers per card, answer or pass, and a wrong guess costs the markers won on that card. No source states that Chytrák is based on it, so treat the link as an inference. Smart10 has more variety than Chytrák: besides true or false it has number, order, century and colour cards.

Game mechanics are free to reuse. Names, question text, artwork and visual identity are not. The plan is an original game in the same genre with its own name, look and questions, and nothing copied from Chytrák or Smart10.

Two ideas that keep weaker players in the game: a catch-up rule where the player in last place picks the next pack, and showing a short fun fact after every card so a wrong answer still ends with something to laugh about.

Question packs

A pack is one file in the repo about one theme, for example "Slovenská príroda" or "Slovenský rap". Before a game, players tick the packs they want. Packs they dislike stay off and the choice is remembered on the device.

One fact list, several formats

Most of your example questions are lists: top 5 songs, fish in Slovakia, highest peaks. Store the list once with its values and some plausible decoys. The game can then ask it as pick-the-true, as an ordering card, as "which is more", or later as "name them all". One researched list gives four or more questions.

Fields that matter

  • source: where the fact comes from. Required. Settles arguments at the table.
  • asOf: date the fact was true. Chart and population questions go stale.
  • fact: one line shown after the card.
  • difficulty 1 to 3 and free tags.
  • note on an option: why a decoy is wrong or surprising.

Adding and importing packs

  • In the repo: add a file under packs/. A build step validates it (schema, duplicate ids, enough decoys, source present) and writes one JSON file per pack plus a small index with a content hash per pack.
  • Local import: drop a pack file into the app, or paste a link to one. It is validated the same way and stored in the browser only. Imported packs can be exported again and sent to a friend.
  • Spreadsheet import for people who will not write YAML: one row per question, pasted as text. Later.
  • In-app editor: a form that produces a pack file. Later, if friends actually want to write packs.

Your two examples

  • Fish in Slovakia: Wikidata cannot answer this. It has no reliable "lives in Slovakia" relation. Use a curated list, for example a national species checklist or a fisheries publication, and write the question by hand with the source cited.
  • Top 5 songs of a Slovak artist: no open dataset. Individual facts ("this song has the most plays as of this date") are not protected, so looking up a handful of artists by hand and citing the source is fine. Copying a chart site wholesale is not. These questions age quickly, hence the asOf field.

Licence consequences

  • Anything translated from OpenTriviaQA or Open Trivia DB must stay CC BY-SA 4.0 with attribution. Question files can carry a different licence from the code.
  • Facts are not copyrightable in the EU, but the wording of a question and the choice of decoys can be. Reuse facts, write your own wording.
  • The EU database right protects against extracting a substantial part of someone's collection. Hand-compiling questions from public facts is fine. This is a research summary and not legal advice.

A realistic content pipeline

  1. Generators: small scripts that query Wikidata and statistics sources and write list questions with values (peaks, rivers, towns, castles, athletes, films, neighbours of countries). Output is reviewed before it is committed.
  2. Translation: filter the English sets for questions that travel well, translate with a language model, convert to the pack format, spot check.
  3. Hand-written Slovak packs: the content that makes the game yours. A language model can draft a pack from a topic and sources, but each fact needs checking against its source because models get local details wrong.
  4. Checks in CI: schema validation, duplicate detection, missing sources, and a warning for asOf dates older than a set age.
  5. Feedback loop: a "report this question" link on every card that opens a prefilled issue.

Architecture

Build in three steps, each usable on its own

STEP 1 · NO NETWORKOne deviceStatic site, packs as JSON, game engine in the browser. Pass and play, solo, teams. Works offline once installed.
STEP 2 · STILL STATICPhones, peer to peerHost device runs the game. Phones join by QR code and connect over WebRTC. Only picks and scores travel between devices.
STEP 3 · ONLY IF NEEDEDSmall relayOne Cloudflare Worker with a Durable Object per room, or a WebSocket relay on your own server. Same messages, different transport.

Why static is enough

  • Data size: a short question is roughly 250 to 300 bytes of JSON. 10,000 questions are about 3 MB raw and an estimated 1 MB compressed. Split by pack, a pack is 50 to 200 KB. No database and no pre-generated decks are needed.
  • Decks from a seed: every device has the same pack files, so the host only sends a seed and the card number. Each device shuffles identically. This keeps network messages to a few bytes and makes the daily card possible without a server.
  • No repeats: the ids of cards you have seen are stored on the device.
  • Offline: a service worker caches the app and each pack on first use. The site becomes installable on a phone home screen, which covers most of what an app would give you.
  • SQLite in the browser was considered and is unnecessary below roughly 100,000 questions.

Friends in one room on the same Wi-Fi is the easy case for peer to peer. The transport sits behind a three-function interface (join, send, on message), so swapping Trystero for a relay later does not touch the game logic.

Code structure

  • Engine: pure TypeScript with no UI. Takes game state and an action, returns new state. Every mode is a reducer like this, which makes rules easy to test and lets the same engine run on one phone, on a host device, or inside a relay.
  • Content: pack schema, validator, and the code that turns a fact list into concrete cards.
  • UI: a small framework and plain CSS, designed for a phone held in one hand.
  • Transport: local (one device), peer to peer, relay.
  • Stack suggestion: TypeScript, Vite, Svelte, built as a static single-page app with a service worker. Preact is an equally good choice if you prefer React-style code.
  • Hosting: GitHub Pages is enough for steps 1 and 2. Cloudflare Pages is the better home if step 3 uses a Worker.
  • App later: an installable web app first. Google Play via a Trusted Web Activity or both stores via Capacitor reuse the same build.
  • Repo layout: packs/, src/engine/, src/content/, src/ui/, src/transport/, scripts/ for generators.

Roadmap

  1. Decide the open questions below that block work: play setup, languages, licences, stack.
  2. Pack format: write the schema and validator, and hand-write two small packs (about 30 cards each) to prove the format before any code depends on it.
  3. Engine and Classic mode on one device, with tests for the scoring rules.
  4. Play it with friends. Adjust rules and difficulty based on one real evening.
  5. Content push: Wikidata generators, translation of filtered English sets, more hand-written Slovak packs. Target a few hundred cards before sharing the link widely.
  6. Polish: offline install, import, Mix mode, statistics, daily card.
  7. Phones: peer-to-peer rooms, then simultaneous modes. Relay only if the peer-to-peer test fails in practice.

Risks

  • Content is the bottleneck. The code is a few weekends. A few thousand good Slovak cards is the long part. A ten-option card needs five plausible decoys, which is more work per question than multiple choice.
  • Wrong facts kill trust. One bad card and the table stops believing the game. Sources on every card, review before merge, and an easy report link are the defences.
  • Translated questions feel foreign. English sets lean on US sport and television. Expect to keep a small share.
  • Stale questions. Charts, populations and records change. The date field and a CI warning keep this visible.
  • Peer-to-peer on phones can fail on mobile data or locked-down Wi-Fi, and stops if the host phone sleeps. Test before relying on it for a party, and keep one-device play as the fallback.
  • Too close to Chytrák or Smart10. Keep the name, look and all questions original.

Open questions for you

  1. How do you actually play? One phone passed around, everyone on their own phone, or a TV with phones as controllers?Recommendation: one phone first, since that is how you play Chytrák today. The answer decides whether network play is version 1 or version 3.
  2. Do you need to play with friends who are not in the room?If yes, the relay moves forward, because peer to peer across mobile networks is the unreliable case.
  3. Which languages? Slovak only, or Slovak and Czech questions mixed, with English later?Recommendation: Slovak interface, each pack tagged with its language, Czech packs allowed.
  4. How close to Chytrák's rules should Classic be? Always 5 true of 10, or a variable number? Does the first wrong pick wipe points, or is it free as in Smart10?Recommendation: variable number of true options, since "always five" lets players count. Both wipe rules as a setting.
  5. Licences. Code under MIT? Question packs under CC BY-SA 4.0?CC BY-SA is required for anything translated from the English sets. Your own packs could be CC0 or CC BY-SA.
  6. Is language-model drafting of questions acceptable, and who reviews?Recommendation: yes for drafts, with every fact checked against its cited source by a person before merge.
  7. Are translated English questions wanted at all, or should the game be hand-made Slovak content plus generated lists only?Skipping them keeps all packs free of the share-alike requirement and avoids the foreign feel, at the cost of volume.
  8. How much content before you call it playable?Suggestion: 200 cards across 8 packs for the first evening, 1,000 before sharing widely.
  9. Time-sensitive questions such as song charts: keep them with a visible "as of" date, or avoid them?
  10. Tone. Is a rude or adults-only pack welcome? Should there be a kids or family filter?
  11. Typed answers. Do you want "name them all" questions, given they need typo-tolerant matching and are slow on a passed-around phone?
  12. Pictures. In scope for the first year, or text only?
  13. Who writes packs? Only you through the repo, friends through pull requests, or friends through an in-app editor and file import?This decides how much effort goes into the editor and the spreadsheet import.
  14. Pack source format. YAML (pleasant to write by hand) or JSON (no build conversion, same as the import format)?Recommendation: YAML in the repo, JSON as the built and imported format, with the importer accepting both.
  15. Stack. Svelte or Preact/React? Any framework you want to learn or avoid?
  16. Hosting and relay. GitHub Pages or Cloudflare Pages? If a relay is ever needed, Cloudflare's free plan or your own server?
  17. Name of the game, and whether you want a custom domain.
  18. Keep the option of an app store release open?It only matters for licence choices now. An installable web app covers the need otherwise.
